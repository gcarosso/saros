Method notes

Universe. Interventional studies with an industry lead sponsor whose primary-completion date falls in calendar 2026–2028, Phase 1 through Phase 3, as registered on ClinicalTrials.gov. The default window is the current month through Dec 2028, the whole registered universe; narrow it in the rail. Registered dates lag real readouts: open-label early-phase programs routinely read out interim data a year or more before the registered date. Dates are sponsor-estimated unless flagged ACTUAL; month-precision dates are placed on the 15th.

Trial health. Enrollment pace is registered enrollment divided by months from start to primary completion; site density is sites per 100 patients. Both are compared with the median of trials in the same area and phase in this universe. A study enrolling below half its peer median pace loses six confidence points unless it is completed or reports an actual date.

rNPV sketch is a deliberately simple model — linear ramp, flat plateau, three-year erosion after exclusivity, single discount rate, one cost-to-complete number — pre-filled from the registry, the literature prior and SEC cover-page share counts. It exists so a number can be stress-tested in the dossier, not to replace a proper model.

Readout confidence is a transparent heuristic, not a model fit: it rewards actual (vs. estimated) completion dates, completed or active-not-recruiting status, actual enrollment counts and recent registry updates, and penalises not-yet-recruiting status, stale records and stopped studies. Use it to triage, not to price.

Decision rules. Each trial carries a next action from an ordered rule list, shown with its trigger: stopped → Pass; cash months < months to readout → Pass / hedge (cash binding); completion date passed or estimated inside 90 days → Wait; ≥2 specialists new last quarter or an insider buy cluster inside 180 days → Review flow; enrollment pace below half the peer median → Diligence enrollment; Phase 3 with firm/guided date and confidence ≥75 → Size / diligence endpoints; other Phase 3 inside 6 months → Diligence endpoints; else Monitor. Date reliability is firm (actual date), estimated (registry estimate), or slipped (primary completion pushed out by a month or more since the previous weekly snapshot, unless the move was off a June/December placeholder or onto an actual date — that is a firming). A slipped date is a Wait: the registry just said the plan changed. Impact = phase weight × (0.5 + literature prior) × size factor (log public float) × firmness × crowding discount, bucketed High ≥0.62 / Med ≥0.35 / Low. All of it is rules, none of it is advice.

Snapshot diff. Each weekly build is compared with the previous one, trial by trial: primary-completion date (with the size of the move in months), estimated → actual, status, enrollment and why-stopped. Slipped, Date firmed and New this snapshot are filterable flags in the rail; the dossier lists the raw before → after values, and each build's diff is kept under data/history. Registry dates lag real readouts, so a single slip mostly confirms what the market has priced; the second slip is the signal.

Base-rate priors are phase-transition probabilities from Wong, Siah & Lo (2019, Biostatistics), oncology vs. non-oncology, shown for context only. They are population averages and say nothing about a specific asset.

Therapeutic area and modality are assigned by pattern-matching titles, conditions, keywords and intervention names. Expect a few percent mis-assignments; the dossier shows the raw fields so you can check.

Sponsor tier. "Large pharma" is a fixed list of ~25 groups with subsidiaries mapped (e.g. Genentech → Roche, Janssen → J&J). "China-domiciled" is inferred from corporate suffixes and city names in the registered sponsor string.

FDA actions come from the openFDA Drugs@FDA endpoint. It covers approval actions on NDAs/BLAs/ANDAs, not target dates, which the FDA does not publish.

Regulatory calendar. PDUFA target dates, advisory-committee meetings and planned resubmissions are read from the companies' own SEC filings: EDGAR full-text search over 8-K, 6-K, 10-Q and 10-K documents and their press-release exhibits for the trailing 24 months, refreshed weekly on the trailing three weeks. Each date is classified from its own sentence, carries day, month or quarter precision (≈ marks a period-end placeholder), and cites the filing it came from; when a later filing states a different date the earlier statement is kept as history and the row is marked moved. Confidence is firm for an exact date in a filing under six months old and guided otherwise. Filers are resolved to tickers by CIK. Coverage trails a paid calendar for companies that only say the date on a call or a slide, and a date inside an image exhibit is invisible to text search. Market cap is proxied by the SEC-reported public float; per-share values use cover-page shares outstanding.

Private companies. Sponsors with no equity linkage — about two thirds of the universe — are resolved by name (current and previous legal names) to SEC Form D issuers in the Biotechnology and Pharmaceuticals industry groups, which gives Regulation D placements since 2019: amount sold per notice, offering size, investor count, and the officers, directors and promoters named on the filing. NIH RePORTER supplies non-dilutive awards by organisation name for the venture-stage tail and for the longevity companies, most of which are pre-IND and absent from ClinicalTrials.gov. Neither source contains valuations, terms, cap tables, burn or board seats; those are stated as unknown rather than estimated.

Programs & peers. The sponsor, not the trial, is the unit of analysis: each program is a vector over area × phase × modality, and peers are the sponsors with the highest cosine similarity on that vector. Trials are coordinates, not oracles — the peer set is how a readout should be read.

Specialist ownership. Latest two 13F-HR information tables for a fixed list of biotech-specialist managers (Baker Bros, Perceptive, RA, OrbiMed, Deerfield, Redmile, Avoro, EcoR1, Logos, Cormorant, Boxer, Vivo, Frazier, Rock Springs, Braidwell, Commodore, Darwin, Paradigm, Bain LS, Vestal Point, Samsara, TCG Crossover, Ally Bridge, Sofinnova, Sectoral, Tang, BVF); multi-strategy managers such as Point72, Viking, Woodline and Polar are deliberately excluded because their books dilute the signal, aggregated by issuer and matched to sponsors by name. This is the "specialist consensus" quality signal; count of holders matters more than dollars, and a 45-day lag applies.

Insider activity. Open-market purchases and sales (Form 4 transaction codes P and S) from the SEC's quarterly insider-transactions data sets, aggregated by issuer over the last two published quarters and joined by issuer CIK or ticker. The current quarter is not yet published, so the window ends at the last quarter-end. FINRA consolidated short interest is shown where the public feed responds.

Longevity companies. Names compiled from public reports (July 2026) are matched to lead-sponsor strings; aging-adjacent trials are flagged by mechanism and indication keywords across all sponsors. Capital raised is as publicly reported and is not re-verified here.

Live refresh queries the ClinicalTrials.gov v2 API for records updated since the snapshot and merges them in place. Hosted viewers sandbox outbound requests; run the local copy for live mode.
